UK drone operator ID label and sticker rules

If your drone needs an operator ID, the ID has to be on the drone itself. Here's what the CAA asks for, in plain English. Checked against the CAA Drone Code in October 2026.

The rules in short

  • Put your operator ID (GBR-OP-…) on every drone you're responsible for. Never your flyer ID.
  • Put it on the main body, where it can be seen from outside, or inside a compartment you can open without tools, like the battery bay.
  • Write it in block capitals taller than 3 mm, clear enough to read.
  • Make it secure and safe from damage, so it won't wash off, fade or peel.
  • You can use the same operator ID on all your drones.

Operator ID or flyer ID?

They're two different things, and only one goes on the drone.

  • Operator ID starts with GBR-OP-. It belongs to the person responsible for the drone, who must be 18 or over. A parent or guardian registers for under-18s. It's renewed every year for a fee: £12.34 in October 2026, but check the current amount on the CAA's registration site. This is the one that goes on the label.
  • Flyer ID starts with GBR-RP-. It shows the pilot passed the free online theory test and lasts 5 years. It does not go on the drone. Many pilots carry a printed card so they can show it if asked.

Where to put the label

  • Outside on the main body: the top shell or the underside of the body works well on most drones.
  • Inside a compartment you can open by hand: under the battery cover or a clip-on hatch. A panel held on with screws doesn't count.
  • Not on parts that come off or wear out: avoid propellers, prop guards, landing gear, gimbal covers and removable batteries. The ID has to stay with the aircraft.

On small drones the battery bay is often the only flat spot big enough. Two-line labels help there.

How big the letters must be

The CAA asks for block capitals taller than 3 mm. That's the height of a capital letter, not the font size. Typical capitals are about 70% of the font size, so text printed at 12 pt is only about 3 mm tall and can fall short. Use at least 14 pt bold if you make your own.

A clear, plain font also matters. Characters like 0 and O or 1 and I should be easy to tell apart. Monospaced fonts help here.

Making it last

The label has to stay readable after rain, grass, crashes and sun.

  • Waterproof vinyl stickers last longest. You can buy A4 vinyl sticker paper for inkjet or laser printers and print the labels at home.
  • A paper label under clear packing tape works well enough to start with.
  • Handwriting on masking tape smudges and peels, so replace it when it does.
  • Clean the surface with a little alcohol first so the label sticks properly.

Remote ID is separate

From 1 January 2026, UK1, UK2 and UK3 class drones must fly with Remote ID switched on. The CAA gives you a Remote ID code under My registration, and your drone's manual explains how to enter it. Remote ID doesn't replace the label. You still need both.

What if you don't label it?

The CAA is clear that flying a drone that needs an operator ID without the label is against the law. The police can check your registration in the field, and a readable ID also helps someone return your drone if it flies away.

Outside the UK

Most of Europe has a similar rule. EU and EEA countries ask you to show your operator registration number on the drone. Size and placement rules vary, so check with your national aviation authority. The label maker accepts operator IDs from any country.
